# Pricing

Private notes on the move off seat-based pricing. The decision is made; this is
about whether I still believe the reasoning.

## The case I made, and the part I under-sold

The public argument was alignment: customers who record more sessions cost us
more, so they should pay more. True, and easy to say.

The part I under-sold internally is that seat pricing was actively teaching
customers to under-adopt. Northwind had eleven seats for a forty-person
engineering org because seats were rationed by their finance team. Every seat we
sold was a person we then had to convince to log in. Usage pricing removes the
gatekeeper.

## What I got wrong

I assumed the migration conversation would be about money. For the three
customers I have spoken to it has been about *predictability* — nobody minds
paying more, everybody minds not knowing the bill in advance. We should have led
with the cap, not the rate.
