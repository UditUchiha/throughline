# Interview loop guide — Backend Engineer

When we are interviewing candidates for the [[Backend Team]], the process is split into four distinct stages to keep things structured without dragging out the timeline. 

First, we start with a standard thirty-minute recruiter chat just to align on basics like compensation expectations, general background, and interest in what we are building with [[Pulse]]. 

Next is the technical screen, which runs for forty-five minutes. This is usually paired with a collaborative coding exercise. We want to see how someone thinks through a problem, handles edge cases, and communicates tradeoffs. Please use standard [[CodeSandbox]] setups for this rather than asking them to configure anything locally from scratch.

If they pass the screen, they move to the final onsite loop, which consists of three core sessions:
1. System Design: Focusing on scalability, data pipelines, and handling high-throughput ingestion.
2. Deep Dive: A review of a past project the candidate worked on, digging into architectural decisions and failure modes.
3. Culture and Collaboration: A casual chat with folks outside the immediate squad, touching on our values and how we handle cross-functional [[Product Roadmap]] changes.

After each interview, please drop your feedback into [[Linear]] within twenty-four hours so the debrief meeting stays productive. Try to focus on specific behavioral and technical observations rather than broad impressions. We want to make sure the candidate has a great experience even if we ultimately pass on them.
